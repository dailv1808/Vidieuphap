## Scene 1 · Hook
- **Visual**: Minh opens a new phone box; a user manual falls out.
- **On-screen**: Where's the manual for your mind?
- **VO**: Your phone came with a user manual. So did your rice cooker. But your mind, the thing you use every single second, where's its manual?

## Scene 2 · Situation
- **Visual**: Minh lying awake at 2 a.m., thought bubbles tangled above his head.
- **On-screen**: Why am I like this?
- **VO**: Ever lie awake at two in the morning, wondering why you snapped at someone that afternoon? Why you knew something was a bad idea and did it anyway? Why some days feel light for no reason, and others feel heavy? We use our minds our whole lives, and almost nobody shows us how they actually work.

## Scene 3 · Open loop
- **Visual**: An ancient palm-leaf book opening, soft light.
- **On-screen**: Over 2,500 years ago
- **VO**: Good news. That manual exists. It was written down more than two and a half thousand years ago. And in a moment you'll see it's so detailed it actually counts the types of mind.

## Scene 4 · Mapping
- **Visual**: Three baskets labeled Discourses, Discipline, Abhidhamma.
- **On-screen**: Three baskets
- **VO**: The Buddha's teachings were collected into three baskets. The first holds the discourses, talks given to specific people in specific situations, like advice from a wise friend. The second holds the rules for monks and nuns. The third is the Abhidhamma. If the discourses are advice, the Abhidhamma is the technical blueprint. It doesn't say "calm down." It takes anger apart: what it's made of, how it arises, and how it ends.

## Scene 5 · Second example
- **Visual**: Split screen: a doctor saying "sleep more" and an X-ray film.
- **On-screen**: Advice vs. X-ray
- **VO**: Think of a doctor's visit. The doctor says sleep earlier and cut back on coffee. That's like the discourses. Easy to follow. But the doctor also has your X-ray, showing every bone. The Abhidhamma is the X-ray of the mind. No advice. Just a clear picture of what's inside.

## Scene 6 · Precise terms
- **Visual**: The word "Abhidhamma" splitting into "Abhi" and "Dhamma".
- **On-screen**: Abhidhamma = the higher teaching
- **VO**: The word is Pali. Abhi means higher, or special. Dhamma means the teaching, or the truth. Together, the higher teaching. Higher not because it's hard, but because it describes things directly, without everyday labels like "me" or "you." The classic beginner's handbook is the Abhidhammattha Sangaha, the Compendium of Abhidhamma. This series follows it, step by step.

## Scene 7 · Common misconception
- **Visual**: A dusty stack of books crossed out; Minh smiling at his reflection in a mirror.
- **On-screen**: Not a dry textbook
- **VO**: People assume the Abhidhamma is only for monks, all charts and lists. It is detailed. But every item in it describes you. Your road rage. Your joy at a compliment. Your midnight cravings. You don't have to believe anything first. Just observe, and check for yourself.

## Scene 8 · Try it
- **Visual**: Minh sipping tea, a tiny label "irritated" floating beside him.
- **On-screen**: Try it: name the feeling
- **VO**: Try this today. When you notice irritation, or joy, or craving, just say it quietly: ah, irritation. Ah, craving. Naming it correctly is page one of the manual.

## Scene 9 · Close
- **Visual**: A wooden table with a big question mark above it.
- **On-screen**: Next: Is your table real?
- **VO**: Next time, a strange question. Is the table in front of you real? The answer changes how you see everything. Follow Inside a Thought so you don't miss it.

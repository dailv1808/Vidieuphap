# Episode 02 · Is your table real?

| | |
|---|---|
| Series | Inside a Thought |
| Topic | Conventional truth (paññatti) and ultimate reality (paramattha) |
| Reference | Abhidhammattha Saṅgaha, ch. I (opening) and ch. VIII (concepts) |

---

## Scene 1 · Hook
- **Visual**: A motorbike parked outside a house.
- **On-screen**: Where is the motorbike?
- **VO**: If we took your motorbike apart, screw by screw, where would "the motorbike" go?

## Scene 2 · Situation
- **Visual**: Parts spread on the floor: wheels, seat, engine, bolts. Minh scratching his head.
- **On-screen**: Which part is "the bike"?
- **VO**: The wheels are here. The seat is over there. The engine's in the corner. Point at each one and ask: is this the motorbike? No, that's a wheel. That's a seat. Not one piece is called "motorbike." Yet put them back together, and everyone calls it a bike. Strange, right?

## Scene 3 · Open loop
- **Visual**: A sticker reading "MOTORBIKE" slapped onto the reassembled parts.
- **On-screen**: A label we stick on
- **VO**: "Motorbike" turns out to be a label we stick on a group of parts. And this way of looking works on something much closer to home. You.

## Scene 4 · Mapping
- **Visual**: Two columns: table, bike, person, city on the left; tiny particles, light, sensations on the right.
- **On-screen**: Labels · Realities
- **VO**: Table, bike, house, even your own name. These are agreements we make so we can talk to each other. Very useful. Without them nobody understands anybody. But look closely, and you only find smaller things. Hardness, heat, color, and the knowing that knows them. None of those depend on what language you call them in.

## Scene 5 · Second example
- **Visual**: Minh in front of a mirror, small labels around him: hair, skin, breath, thoughts, feelings.
- **On-screen**: Where is "Minh"?
- **VO**: Now try it on yourself. Where is the person called Minh? In the hair? Cut it, and Minh is still Minh. In the thoughts? They change every minute. In the feelings? Happy in the morning, sad by evening. The harder you look, the more you find only small, changing pieces, with one name stuck on all of them.

## Scene 6 · Precise terms
- **Visual**: Two boxes: "Concept – Paññatti" and "Ultimate reality – Paramattha".
- **On-screen**: Concept ≠ Ultimate reality
- **VO**: The Abhidhamma calls the first kind concepts, in Pali, pannatti. Conventional truth: names, shapes and ideas like person, bike, table. The second kind is paramattha, ultimate reality. What remains when every label is gone, things with their own nature. Fire is hot whether you call it fire in English or Vietnamese. The Abhidhamma mostly studies ultimate realities. And once you see this, you'll notice "I" is also a concept, laid over a flow of mind and body.

## Scene 7 · Common misconception
- **Visual**: Minh riding the reassembled bike, smiling.
- **On-screen**: Concepts still matter
- **VO**: A common mistake is thinking: so everything is an illusion and nothing matters. Not at all. We need concepts to live, to work, to love each other. The Buddha used words like "I" and "you" too. The difference is using labels without being trapped by them.

## Scene 8 · Try it
- **Visual**: Minh's palm on a table, small ripples reading "hard", "cool".
- **On-screen**: Try it: touch without naming
- **VO**: Try it. Put your hand on a table. Don't think "table." Just feel. Hard. Cool. Smooth. You just touched ultimate reality, a little.

## Scene 9 · Close
- **Visual**: Four glowing squares with question marks.
- **On-screen**: Next: the four ultimate realities
- **VO**: So what is ultimately real? The Abhidhamma says only four things. Next episode. Follow Inside a Thought.

## Key terms
| English | Pāli | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Concept, conventional truth | Paññatti / Sammuti | Truth by agreement: names and ideas |
| Ultimate reality | Paramattha (dhamma) | Truth with its own intrinsic nature, independent of names |

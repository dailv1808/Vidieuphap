# Episode 03 · The four ultimate realities

| | |
|---|---|
| Series | Inside a Thought |
| Topic | Mind (citta), mental factors (cetasika), matter (rūpa), Nibbāna |
| Reference | Abhidhammattha Saṅgaha, ch. I, opening verse ("the ultimate realities are fourfold") |

---

## Scene 1 · Hook
- **Visual**: A steaming bowl of noodle soup on a wooden table.
- **On-screen**: Everything = 4 things?
- **VO**: What if everything you experience, from this morning's breakfast to last night's argument, came down to just four things?

## Scene 2 · Situation
- **Visual**: Minh eating, small labels: "bowl", "sweet taste", "love this", "knowing the taste".
- **On-screen**: What's in a bowl of soup?
- **VO**: Minh eats a bowl of noodle soup. There's the bowl, the broth, the noodles. That's matter. There's the knowing of the taste. Sweet. Spicy. Then pleasure, liking, wanting a second bowl. And once he's truly full, a brief moment of wanting nothing at all. Hold on to that picture.

## Scene 3 · Open loop
- **Visual**: The bowl separating into four glowing layers.
- **On-screen**: Four layers
- **VO**: That one bowl of soup holds all four ultimate realities. Let's peel them apart.

## Scene 4 · Mapping
- **Visual**: Four cards flipping: Knowing · Its flavors · Matter · Extinguishing.
- **On-screen**: Knowing · Flavors · Matter · Extinguishing
- **VO**: Layer one is the knowing of the taste. Just knowing. Layer two is what comes along with the knowing: pleasure, liking, wanting more. Like spices in the broth. Layer three is matter: the bowl, the soup, and your tongue. Layer four is harder to picture. That full, wanting-nothing moment is only a distant hint, because the wanting comes back. Layer four is the complete extinguishing of craving, never to return.

## Scene 5 · Second example
- **Visual**: Minh on a motorbike in traffic; a horn drawn as red sound waves.
- **On-screen**: A traffic jam has 4 layers too
- **VO**: One more. You're stuck in traffic and someone leans on the horn behind you. The horn and your ear are matter. Knowing "there's a horn" is mind. The unpleasant feeling and the irritation are mental factors. All three arise in a flash. And the fourth layer, extinguishing, is the reason people study all the others.

## Scene 6 · Precise terms
- **Visual**: Four squares with English, Pāli and counts.
- **On-screen**: Mind · Mental factors · Matter · Nibbāna
- **VO**: The four ultimate realities are: one, mind, citta in Pali, the knowing of an object. Two, mental factors, cetasika, the states that arise with the mind. There are fifty-two, such as feeling, greed, aversion, faith and wisdom. Three, matter, rupa, with twenty-eight types. Four, Nibbana, the extinguishing of greed, hatred and delusion. The first three are conditioned. They arise and pass away. Nibbana alone is unconditioned.

## Scene 7 · Common misconception
- **Visual**: A cloud-heaven lightly crossed out; next to it, a candle just blown out, a thin wisp of smoke.
- **On-screen**: Nibbāna is not a place
- **VO**: Many people imagine Nibbana as a heaven, a beautiful place to go. In the Abhidhamma it isn't a place at all. The word means blowing out, like a flame going out when the fuel runs out. It's the end of greed, hatred and delusion, not a destination.

## Scene 8 · Try it
- **Visual**: Minh holding a coffee, three tiny labels: matter, mind, mental factor.
- **On-screen**: Try it: three layers
- **VO**: At your next meal, try separating three layers. This is matter. This is the knowing of taste. This is the liking. Three different things, happening at once.

## Scene 9 · Close
- **Visual**: A small light blinking on and off rapidly.
- **On-screen**: Next: What is the mind?
- **VO**: Next, we start with the first reality: the mind. You'll be surprised how fast it is. Follow Inside a Thought.
